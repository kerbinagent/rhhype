# Causal short-horizon spread markout experiment

## Why the current forecast misses the holding horizon

The `convergence` gate subtracts the median **level** of executable closing spread observed during the preceding 900 seconds from the current opening signal. It does not ask what a route's closing spread tends to be about ten seconds after a wide entry quote. The trade is selected during an unusual opening basis, so an unconditional historical level can be far below the closing basis that persists through the hold.

The retained CASHCAT paired trade at 16:44:12 UTC illustrates the gap. Its trigger forecast was +$1.880, using a historical closing spread of -$0.070. The delayed fills established a $3.956 opening spread, but the eventual executable closing spread was $3.992: price P&L was -$0.036, followed by $0.895 fees and $0.499 other modeled cost, for -$1.431 net. The conservative CASHCAT trade five seconds later forecast +$0.604 against a $0.225 historical closing spread; its actual closing spread was $3.715 and net result -$2.704. These are two observations, not a calibrated return estimate. Most earlier median-policy closes were one-leg failures, which must be assessed separately from paired markouts.

## Proposed read-only forecast comparison

1. Reuse the route-specific rolling closing-spread samples and keep the existing executable-depth, freshness, skew, source-advancement, and Standard-fee rules. Extend each bounded sample with its actual quantity, long entry value, both source timestamps, both receipt timestamps, and stream generations. The present `(tick time, bps)` tuples alone cannot prove that a future observation is 12 seconds newer at **both** sources or that it quotes the original quantity.
2. Predeclare a **12-second decision-to-close horizon**, reflecting the observed roughly 12-second trigger-to-final-close time under the ten-second post-open exit request. For an anchor sample, use the first valid paired future sample whose **both source timestamps** are at least 12 seconds beyond the respective anchor source timestamps and whose receipt time is within 12–16 seconds of the anchor decision. Keep the same generations, reject any intervening data gap, and record absent outcomes as censored. Do not interpolate or backfill a missing book.
3. For a decision at time `t`, train only on anchor/outcome pairs whose outcome was fully observed **before `t`**. Let `C_t` be the current executable closing spread in bps and `Δ_i = C_outcome,i − C_anchor,i`. Compare three frozen forecasts: the existing unconditional median level, persistence (`C_t`), and `C_t + median(Δ_i)` from the route's prior mature pairs. The last is a horizon-aware spread forecast, not a cash-profit prediction. Current `C_t` may be used as a known feature without allowing that sample to train its own forecast.
4. Limit training to the existing 900-second/900-observation/2,000-route bounds. Use anchors at least 12 seconds apart, but do **not** treat their outcomes as independent: a 12-second anchor can mature at 16 seconds while the next anchor begins at 12 seconds. Require at least 40 mature anchors spanning at least 480 seconds for a route; otherwise show warmup. A valid fresh paired quote may resolve a pending anchor even if it arrives within the one-second sampling interval; it does not become a new training observation or anchor. Retain no full books for this comparison. Publish route-level anchor, match, score, pending, and censor counts alongside model error.
5. Score predictions strictly forward in time: closing-spread mean signed error, mean absolute error, and root mean squared error in bps. Keep retained-route lifetime metrics separate from active-generation and rolling-window counts. For comparisons and uncertainty, block observations by physical venue pair and wall-clock periods longer than the 16-second outcome deadline: opposite directed routes share books and nearby anchors may overlap. Keep these quote markouts separate from actual paired-fill rate, entry drift, four fees, and realized paper P&L. The first comparison should run without a new trading gate or threshold tuning.

The current series normalizes each observation using **its own** quantity and entry value. That is enough for an exploratory basis-level comparison, but it is not an original-quantity $1,000 execution forecast. A gate would need to requote the future closing book at the anchor's quantity and separately model the probability and cost of obtaining both entry fills. No maker fills are inferred.

## Confirmation diagnostic before changing its gate

The separate `confirmed` policy may lose a candidate to its existing one-second source-skew gate **before** the candidate's one-second confirmation due time. At arm, the Hyperliquid source clock can already be roughly 0.5–0.8 seconds behind receipt while the other venue updates faster. The targeted Hyperliquid refresh currently begins at the due time, so a priority-4 refresh slot only helps candidates that survive until then. This is a hypothesis, not an observed terminal count. Use the new distinct lifecycle reasons and per-side waiting clocks to measure pre-due skew losses, source advancement, refresh dispatch, and expiry before adjusting eligibility or timing.

## Predeclared model version 2, for a separate pilot

Model version 2 adds a fourth quote-only forecast without changing the three original forecasts. At each anchor, fit the route's **strictly prior mature** pairs `(C_anchor, C_future)` from the same 900-second window. Let `n` be their count, `meanΔ = mean(C_future − C_anchor)`, and `β_OLS = covariance(C_anchor,C_future) / variance(C_anchor)`. Set `β = 1 + [n/(n+20)](β_OLS − 1)` and predict

`C_future = C_now + meanΔ + (β − 1)(C_now − mean(C_anchor))`.

The **20 pseudo-observations** shrink the slope toward persistence (`β=1`) and are fixed before outcomes. If anchor variance is degenerate, use `C_now + meanΔ`; nonfinite or extreme results fall back to a finite persistence-based value. All four forecasts share the existing **40 mature anchors spanning 480 seconds** warmup, 12–16-second first-valid outcome window, and unchanged source/generation/gap censoring. Nearby anchor intervals can overlap by up to four seconds. Closing-spread inputs above **1,000,000 bps absolute** are rejected as invalid unit/price observations. These guards are fixed for the pilot.

The snapshot now reports bps error separately for each directed route and exports at most **5,000** recent mature anchor/outcome rows, including both timestamps, both bps values, quantities, entry values, source times, and the frozen prediction made at the anchor. It distinguishes bounded retained-route lifetime counters from the active generation segment and its current pending/rolling-window counts. This supports later chronological reanalysis without reconstructing future values at decision time. The export cap and per-route metric cap are hard bounds; global metrics can hide route differences and should not be used alone.

Run version 2 into a **new output directory** and compare only predictions collected after that pilot starts. Processes already running the version-1 module keep their loaded code and observations; their old snapshots cannot retrospectively validate the fourth forecast. No model result is a $1,000 fixed-quantity execution forecast or a trading-gate recommendation.
