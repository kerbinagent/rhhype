## Carry the whole position, not just an opening spread

Let signed base positions be `R` on RH and `H` on HL, with positive meaning long. Track separate USDG and USDC synthetic cash ledgers, FIFO entry lots and their actual prices/fees, accrued funding, margin buffers, outstanding quote/cancel/hedge intents, and `delta=R+H`. A hedged long pair has `R=q, H=-q`: delta is zero, but gross perp inventory is `2q`; basis, funding and liquidation exposure persist on both venues. [HL's mark price determines margin and liquidation](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/robust-price-indices), and [HL funding settles hourly](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/funding). RH's [Lighter domain has separate sequencing and liquidity from Core](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain/lighter-domains/), so transfer or collateral netting between venues is not implied.

For one **fully closed** RH maker-buy → HL taker-sell → RH maker-sell → HL taker-buy lot `q`, the conditional cycle identity is

`Π_fee_only = q[(p_RH,sell − p_RH,buy) − (p_HL,buyback − p_HL,sell)] − Σ four actual-notional fees + funding_RH + funding_HL − actual conversion/transfer costs.`

Record RH and HL cash separately first. Any combined USD figure is a conditional **USDG=USDC parity comparison**, not executable conversion. Each buy/sell creates or reduces a real perp position; opposite exposure is closed only by a recorded opposite-side fill. Use FIFO when an RH exit reduces an older RH lot, and reduce the matching HL lot only when its HL buyback is modeled as filled. If an RH maker sell `x` exceeds existing matched RH long and HL short, `min(x,R,−H)` is a reduction and the excess is a **new reverse pair or unhedged short**; forbid that excess in the first experiment. The short-pair signs reverse.

At any time define `L(R,H)` as current separate cash plus **size-walked, first-eligible taker liquidation** of the actual `R` and `H`, minus their own exit fees, accrued financing and any modeled conversion. Missing depth makes `L` unknown, never zero. Quote decisions compare a proposed action's *marginal change in this entire inventory value* against an explicit fallback. For an existing long RH/short HL pair, a proposed RH maker-sell `x` followed by HL taker-buy `x` competes with closing both `x` lots immediately by taker:

`incremental_advantage ≈ RH_maker_sell_proceeds − RH_maker_fee − delayed_HL_buy_cost − HL_taker_fee − [RH_taker_sell_proceeds − RH_taker_fee − immediate_HL_buy_cost − HL_taker_fee] − wait_risk.`

The old entry prices and entry fees cancel **in this marginal comparison** because they are already incurred. They remain in the FIFO ledger and the completed cycle identity. At unchanged HL prices, RH Standard's explicit maker/taker fees are both zero under the frozen public schedule, so a maker exit can improve the *RH exit price* by some displayed spread. It cannot erase the initial HL taker fee, the eventual HL buyback fee, basis movement, or failure to fill. [Hummingbot's documented cross-exchange strategy](https://github.com/hummingbot/hummingbot-site/blob/main/docs/strategies/v1-strategies/cross-exchange-market-making.md) also hedges actual maker fills even when the hedge became unattractive; its supported spot workflow is an operational analogy, not proof of perp profitability. The inventory/market-order trade-off is formalized by [Guilbaud and Pham](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.5040), while [Guéant, Lehalle and Fernandez-Tapia](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.3115) explicitly constrain inventory risk. Their parameters do not transfer to these venues without measurement.

Inventory recycling can **amortize a real fixed USDG/USDC rebalance or transfer cost** if collateral stays prefunded and many cycles occur between transfers. It cannot amortize a proportional trading fee, eliminate the currency basis, or justify moving a 5 bp stress reserve into cash as though paid. Report: (1) fill-notional cash and actual fee-only result; (2) funding and financing; (3) any modeled transfer/conversion *when it occurs*; and (4) a separate 5 bp reserve stress and target. Keep per-venue cash and prefunding capacity visible throughout.

## Simple next paper policy

Use BTC/ETH direct perp controls and NVDA/XAG as separate RWA cohorts; preserve equivalent base-unit, collateral and asset-specific fee checks. Keep **$100/$250/$500/$1,000**, with $1,000 primary and no below-$100 search. Each size/policy/tier is its own ledger and cannot be summed with another branch. Freeze a fresh **post-market replication** and a distinct **regular-session NVDA window**; do not pool them. The current post-market sample has sparse equity flow despite 24-hour volume eligibility. A regular session may provide more flow but narrower quotes, with no predetermined net benefit. Treat XAG by its own time-of-day activity and BTC/ETH as 24/7 controls.

1. **Entry and hedge.** Start one pair per asset with the existing quote/queue, source-and-receipt, generation, tick/lot, processing-delay, fee and prefund guards. A public print at/through the quote is only possible-flow evidence. On each attributed RH maker-buy increment, request an equal HL taker-sell at the first eligible delayed book. If the partial is below a valid hedge lot, retain it as unresolved RH inventory; a separately frozen dust rule may allow at most **$20 unhedged notional and one second**, then request a fallback flatten. Do not mark the full quote filled because one increment traded.
2. **Two exit policies on the same candidate IDs.** Control A requests taker flatten of both actual positions at ten seconds after the first completed entry hedge, as in the pilot. Policy B, immediately after a matched pair exists, posts **at most one RH maker sell** for no more than matched inventory, at the smallest valid ask tick that clears a *pretrained conditional full-cycle threshold* and stays within a frozen quote-distance cap. A fixed-best-ask B control isolates the threshold's effect. Its prospective threshold includes the conditional HL buyback after flow receipt plus delay, both remaining fees, funding/financing to the deadline, a risk allowance and the $0.10 target; it must also clear a fee-only screen. If no quote meets the cap, B simply holds the pair for the frozen deadline and then requests taker flatten. It does not retrospectively select a better ask.
3. **Exit flow and hedge.** Only subsequent RH **buy-aggressor** trades at/through the outstanding ask can consume same-price displayed queue ahead; a trade touch is never an actual fill. Each attributed RH sell `x` reduces the RH long lot, creates `delta=−x` until a corresponding HL taker **buy** fills, and triggers that buy after local flow-confirmation receipt plus the frozen HL delay. If HL depth/limit/minimum fails, keep an explicit unhedged residual and request emergency flatten; do not drop the case. The symmetric path handles an existing RH short/HL long by RH maker buy then HL taker sell. Cancellation-pending fills are obligations.
4. **Deadlines and risk.** Policy B keeps the **ten-second request deadline** as the primary like-for-like test; at that time cancel the passive exit, reconcile late fills, and request taker liquidation for actual residuals. Ten seconds is a request, not assured flat time. A separate 60-second research branch tests whether more passive-exit opportunities compensate for funding, basis variance, collateral use, stale books and tails. Crossing an hourly funding event requires sourced cash on both venues or an unknown outcome, never a projected APR plugged in as realized cash. Do not reclassify ten-second losses as wins because a later quote improves. Hard stops: invalid/gapped feed, excessive source/receipt skew, no full residual-depth quote, margin headroom below frozen buffer, gross inventory above one original pair, unhedged notional above $20 or one second, or an unknown cancel/partial ordering. Record failure and residual position rather than a fabricated close. No uncapped hold is allowed.

The **baseline hedge trigger is immediate after each attributed valid-lot maker increment**. A cost-aware alternative may delay only subminimum dust *inside* the $20/one-second hard band when a model trained before the test predicts that avoiding an HL taker spread/fee is worth more than a conservative bound on adverse move, emergency unwind and exposure time. Its own branch must count every extra unhedged second and all eventual hedge or flatten fees; if the model is unready, the first valid immediate hedge or emergency flatten wins. Simply omitting the HL hedge from the ledger is not a saving. Waiting for a second RH flow is an inventory bet, not a zero-risk offset.

The quote model should be trained on **flow-conditioned** RH opposite-side trades and the first later HL book; it should track book state, queue ahead, spread, volatility and session. The [queue-reactive model](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.0563) motivates conditioning on book state, but public trades cannot identify our counterfactual queue position or actual fill probability. If fewer than the frozen minimum number and time span of matched observations are available for an asset/session, adaptive B abstains; fixed-best B remains descriptive. Freeze the calibration gates, quote-distance cap, ten-second fallback, 60-second sensitivity and handling of partials **before a new capture**. Existing post-market outcomes make any reanalysis exploratory. A new prospective run should report every original candidate and independent control, including no quote, no flow, partial flow, failed hedge, failed cancel, stale feed, forced exit, and open inventory. Compare matched **complete** cycles only alongside the full denominator and unresolved fraction; never assign a missing exit zero P&L or infer a portfolio return by summing correlated branches.

### What would change the decision

The research route is worth extending only if a new, session-specific paper run shows a material number of conditional **complete** cycles whose fee-only margins survive coupled flow-to-hedge moves, then remain positive under the stated reserve/financing stress, while unresolved and emergency-exit exposure stays within predeclared limits. If the second RH maker side rarely gets possible flow, if first-eligible HL buybacks erase its spread, or if regular-session spreads compress as arrival intensity rises, passive exits merely extend risk. Actual fill probability, queue priority, cancel acknowledgements, funding and USDG/USDC conversion execution remain unavailable from this public capture. Any live conclusion would require a separately authorized private execution study.

The most discriminating **public-data falsification** is XAG Standard's passive-exit path: its static fee-only margin is small and its stressed screen already negative, so first-eligible future HL buyback and RH exit-flow coverage can reject it quickly. NVDA's static fee-only median is already below zero; test it separately in regular equity hours only if the entry/exit quote or basis distribution changes enough to clear a frozen full-cycle threshold. Native BTC/ETH face roughly 9 bp of two-leg HL taker charges, and their completed fixed-best pilot cycles lost money; keep them as continuous-feed controls instead of treating the same entry spread as a new opportunity.
