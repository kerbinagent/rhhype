# Why did Standard fixed-best BTC/ETH receive no attributed entry flow?

**Stopped-pilot diagnostic only.** This uses the completed 21:21–22:11 UTC RH maker public capture, its [cached audit](../data/derived/rh-small-maker-v1-cached/audit.jsonl.gz), and [cached lifecycle readout](../reports/rh-small-maker-v1/CACHED-RESULTS.md). It does not read the current passive-exit capture, change a frozen policy, place an order, or infer private fills. The cached implementation's full equivalence to the original replay was still pending in its published readout. The bounded [offline diagnostic](../scripts/analyze_rh_entry_queue.py) verifies the cached audit SHA and stopped raw gzip SHA and retains [machine-readable counts](../reports/rh-entry-queue-followup/diagnostics.json).

## The first explanation is unequal observable time

At the primary **$1,000 fixed-best bid**, both tiers submitted quotes at the then-current RH best bid (`join`). Standard BTC ended in a `trade_cancel_order_ambiguous` state after **25 activations and 24 closed no-flow episodes**; Standard ETH ended at `eligible_flow_before_queue_snapshot` after **33 activations and 32 closed no-flow episodes**. The branch conservatively stops at its first unresolved obligation, even with reported positions flat. The known Standard active-queue exposure was only **37.925 s BTC** and **46.460 s ETH**, spread across roughly **96.27 s** and **124.31 s** from first decision to terminal uncertainty. Thus “zero Standard fills versus eight/two Premium fills” compares different observable windows.

| Standard BTC | 25 | 37.925 s | 0.11187 BTC | 2 | 0 | Trade/cancel order |

| Standard ETH | 33 | 46.460 s | 1.3027 ETH | 7 | 0 | Flow before queue snapshot |

Within **each Standard branch's own first-decision-to-terminal interval**, Premium BTC had **28 activations and zero maker increments**, versus Standard BTC's 25 and zero. Premium ETH had **35 activations and one maker increment/filled episode**, versus Standard ETH's 33 and zero. The other **eight BTC and one ETH** Premium filled episodes occurred after the corresponding Standard branch had already censored itself. The published [RH account schedule](https://apidocs.rh.lighter.xyz/docs/account-types.md) gives Standard **200 ms maker/cancel processing** and Premium base **0 ms**, with 100 ms modeled client/network allowance in the pilot. Timing could contribute, but the stopped data do **not** isolate latency as the cause of the 8-versus-0 BTC difference. Premium also pays positive explicit fees, and its completed filled episodes lost money in the cached replay.

## Queue and cancellations

The median same-price queue ahead at activation was about **9.3×** a $1,000 BTC order in Standard (median order ≈0.01198 BTC) and **3.5×** a $1,000 ETH order (median order ≈0.3734 ETH). These are medians across distinct moments, not a fixed queue or a guarantee of priority. Only **2/25** Standard BTC and **6/33** Standard ETH activation snapshots had zero same-price ahead. Premium saw zero same-price ahead in **19/147 BTC** and **12/68 ETH** activations. Better-priced displayed demand is not subtracted a second time from an at/through trade, consistent with the frozen queue rule; observed cancellations ahead receive no queue credit.

Best-bid changes drove **21/25** Standard BTC cancellation requests and **27/32** Standard ETH requests. Standard had **zero post-only rejects**; Premium BTC had one among 148 quote requests and Premium ETH had zero. Standard's median receipt-to-cancel-or-unknown activated interval was **0.850 s BTC** and **0.901 s ETH**, despite the five-second maximum rest. The 200 ms published maker/cancel processing plus the model's network allowance and next eligible public-book confirmation make short top-of-book life and cancellation races material. Censorship from trade/cancel or queue-initialization timing was present in both tiers; the audit cannot identify a private ACK or actual queue position.

## Was one RH price tick better enough to access more flow?

The diagnostic joins each frozen quote to the latest prior valid RH book in the **stopped** raw archive and examines a hypothetical bid **one valid RH tick above** the requested price: **+$0.1 BTC** (about **0.012 bp** near $83,400) or **+$0.01 ETH** (about **0.037 bp** near $2,676). All **25 Standard BTC and 33 Standard ETH** quote intervals remained below the recorded RH ask at both decision and activation; this only checks those two instants, not whether an altered order would remain passive later. The improved price had **zero displayed same-price quantity ahead** at activation in **24/25 BTC** and **29/33 ETH** intervals. At $1,000, the direct bid-price concession is only about **$0.0012 BTC** or **$0.0037 ETH** per entry before any altered flow or adverse selection.

Historical sell prints supply little support for materially more flow at that one-tick band. In Standard BTC's active intervals, two source-valid seller prints were observed: one at/through the old bid and **one 0.0002 BTC print ($16–17)** exactly one tick above it. That extra-band print arrived at the **same receipt as Standard BTC's terminal trade/cancel ambiguity**; it is not a clean fill opportunity. Standard ETH had seven at/through-old-bid seller prints and **zero** additional prints in `(old bid, old bid + one tick]`. Premium BTC had one extra-band 0.0002 BTC print in a later, closed interval; Premium ETH had none. The one-tick price band did not add an observed **full $1,000 quantity** in either Standard branch. Same-price queue ahead, source timing, and partial obligations still matter even when a print reaches the hypothetical price. [The detailed diagnostic](../reports/rh-entry-queue-followup/diagnostics.json) separately records price-band volumes, trade IDs, source/receipt times, and censor reasons.

The observed median seller-trade price distance from the standing bid was **+0.006 bp BTC and 0 bp ETH** in Standard's small number of source-valid active-interval prints; Premium's differently sampled longer intervals had **−0.012 bp BTC and −0.560 bp ETH**. Negative means a sell print at or below the bid. This describes market prints *conditional on the quote's public observation window*, not the price, size, or quality of a private maker fill. In particular, the Standard BTC terminal print should remain ambiguous, even though a simple receipt-window diagnostic can locate its trade price.

## Next frozen question, not a changed result

A prospective follow-up could predeclare a fixed-best bid versus **one-tick inside-spread** bid on the *same fresh stream*, with the same asset, size, activation/cancel delays, exact RH and HL lot/minimum checks, first advanced activation book, queue-ahead rule, and full hedge/exit liabilities. It should record time at a valid passive price, source-valid at/through flow, partial queue exhaustion, post-only rejections, terminal uncertainty, and **all-cost completed net** for both policies on an all-entry denominator. It should stop or censor when the improved price crosses the ask or the book/feed is stale, including during cancellation pending. A predeclared matched shared-time cohort would separate the tier's different exposure lengths from its processing delay. No historical print here is promoted to a counterfactual fill, and this note does not retune the ongoing passive-exit study.
