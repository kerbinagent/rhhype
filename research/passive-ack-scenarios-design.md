# Hypothetical ACK scenarios for a future RH paper study

**Future-only design and synthetic implementation; no capture or archive replay launched.** Keep the running passive-exit v1 capture, its 18 frozen sources, the separate 20-source retirement correction, and their results untouched. The new [scenario subclass](../scripts/rh_passive_ack_scenarios.py) and [synthetic fixtures](../tests/test_rh_passive_ack_scenarios.py) prepare a separate sensitivity study on public events. An ACK here is a declared counterfactual clock assumption, not an observed private order acknowledgement or a claim of actual execution. The [public queue note](public-queue-uncertainty-followup.md) explains the stopped Standard BTC/ETH censor evidence.

## Why a small adapter is possible

The entry quote has modeled `activation_due_ns` and `cancel_due_ns`, but [MakerBranch._on_book](../scripts/rh_maker_engine.py) currently sets `activated_ns`, `activated_source_ns`, same-price queue ahead, and `canceled_ns` only on later RH books (lines 534–551). Its [_on_trade](../scripts/rh_maker_engine.py) then censors eligible flow between due and that first activation book (lines 638–662), or after cancel due before book confirmation (lines 681–693). [PassiveExitBranch](../scripts/rh_passive_exit_engine.py) repeats those public-book transitions for the exit ask (lines 315–341) and the corresponding trade censors (lines 424–475). Both engines call `tick(receipt_ns)` **before** exposing each incoming event to the branch (entry `process`, lines 804–832), and the passive class also calls inherited `tick` first. This creates a narrow extension point: set the assumed ACK state from **previously received** public information immediately before inherited event processing. Once set, inherited book handlers skip their activation/cancel blocks; inherited trade/hedge/exit/cash mechanics remain in force.

The smallest useful implementation is one new `AssumedAckPassiveExitBranch`, covering both `control10s` and passive-ask exit policies; the existing passive class already inherits the guarded maker-buy engine. A separate entry engine or fill-accounting implementation is unnecessary. It needs more than a `tick` override: shared activation/cancel helpers, cancellation request hooks, ordered timer advancement, a raw public anchor, source-revision checks, bounded duplicate-ID evidence, and scenario-labelled audit/summary fields. No monkeypatch of the frozen classes or mutation of shared `Book` instances. The existing entry and passive ask tombstones remain mandatory; a synthetic ACK cannot let old-source flow escape after retirement.

### Separate correction for a current frozen-v1 boundary defect

This is distinct from the proposed ACK interpretation. [LateFlowGuardMixin.process](../scripts/rh_maker_late_flow_guard.py:101) checks retired entry quotes **before** [MakerBranch.process](../scripts/rh_maker_engine.py:804) calls `tick`. A qualifying delayed trade can cause the quote to retire in that tick, after the guard check. The subsequent `_on_trade` sees no entry quote and drops the evidence, leaving a falsely known no-flow episode. Moreover, the guard's [matching predicate](../scripts/rh_maker_late_flow_guard.py:93) uses `receipt > retired_ns`; merely checking again still misses a tombstone retired at that same receipt.

The standalone [retirement correction](../scripts/rh_passive_retirement_guard.py) extends matching to the equal-receipt case and checks after inherited processing. It then invokes the inherited unknown-marking path, without fabricating a fill or changing economic assumptions. The [review tests](../tests/test_passive_retirement_review.py) reproduce frozen v1's known-zero error and verify corrected control/passive behavior. The frozen safety invariant is an explicit expected failure until a separately labelled correction/adjudication is used. Running capture and its 18-source protocol remain unchanged. Any future ACK branch must include this correction, not bury it inside an ACK feature or relabel its effect as a latency benefit.

## Predeclared scenarios and causal clock

Run independent ledgers with fixed scenario IDs and latency values declared before a future prospective capture. The implemented minimal set is `assumed_ack_base` (the same final normalized tier-specific maker and cancel due delays as strict v1) and `assumed_ack_plus_200ms` (200 ms added to both maker and cancellation delays); strict v1 and its separately labelled retirement correction remain controls. Clone the already normalized configuration rather than accidentally rerunning Premium's default-delay remapping when a stress equals 300 ms. These scenarios move due times without changing price policy, order size, fee tier, or hedge/exit rules. Do not select delays from the holdout. A stopped replay after this design is exploratory and must disclose its later design/freeze time. The stressed branch remains an assumption scenario, and a shorter delay is not automatically an optimistic profit case: an earlier fill can lose money.

At each due time (d), a synthetic timer transition may be processed at the first subsequent **receipt** callback, but must be stamped `assumed_effective_ns=d`, not its later processing receipt. It may use only RH books already received at or before (d); the incoming event is invisible because `process` calls `tick` first. Prefer the latest such book in the same feed generation. Require `source_ns <= received_ns <= d`, source and receipt ages within `max_book_age_ns`, valid positive depth, and no previously observed invalidation. A book received after (d), even if its source predates (d), cannot initialize this scenario retrospectively. Even a current book received exactly at (d) is invisible to that callback's timer; stable capture sequence resolves receipt ties. If no qualifying anchor exists, mark `ack_anchor_unavailable` unknown; do not silently fall back to a later favorable book. A generation change, source/receipt reversal, or a gap while an economic obligation is open remains unknown.

Keep a branch-local **raw RH anchor** separately from `self.books`: [PassiveExitBranch._on_book](../scripts/rh_passive_exit_engine.py:304) makes accounting copies and depletes their displayed depth after synthetic executions. Those depleted books are unsuitable as untouched public insertion snapshots. Cache valid raw RH levels, source/receipt, generation and event identity before delegated processing, and clear on invalidation. One latest anchor is sufficient only if every timer due is processed before accepting a newer snapshot.

For the **entry bid**, check the original quote price/quantity against RH grid, size, notional, and the anchor's ask: `price < best_ask` for hypothetical post-only acceptance. Set `activated_ns=d`, `activated_source_ns=d`, and `generation=anchor.generation`. Initialize `ahead_same` from displayed bids **at the quoted price** in the anchor, and keep `ahead_better` diagnostic only. For the **passive exit ask**, use the same method with `price > best_bid` and displayed asks at the quoted price. Existing decision-time admissibility and future actual-order-rule checks stay in effect. The queue model explicitly assumes that anchor's same-price displayed quantity is still ahead at (d). It gives no credit for anonymous cancellations or for pre-due trades; additions between anchor source and (d) could make the real queue larger, so this is a *point scenario*, not a conservative fill bound. After (d), only deduplicated, source-valid and receipt-valid aggressor prints at/through the quote deplete same-price ahead and then the remaining original quantity. The print caps any attributed increment, and fills use the **own quote price**, never the print price.

The implementation conservatively stops with `assumed_post_only_reject_unresolved` when the due-time anchor would reject either direction. The inherited entry and ask handlers have different unactivated rejection lifecycles. Stopping avoids additional trade/book-gate overrides, preserves passive inventory and prevents a later book from reactivating a rejected request. Rejection is not converted into completed zero net.

For cancellation, an already requested quote receives `canceled_ns=cancel_due_ns` once the assumed effective time has passed; do this for both entry and passive ask **before** inherited `_on_trade` on the current receipt. Preserve the original request and due timestamps in audit. An eligible print with `source_ns < cancel_due_ns` can still consume the old quote even if received after the assumed ACK; `source_ns > cancel_due_ns` cannot. A print at the exact due timestamp remains `cancel_fill_same_source_time`/passive equivalent **unknown** under the inherited tie rule. Do not rewrite it as no fill. Keep the quote and its remaining quantity through the inherited `cancel_due + max_book_age` late-print window; after retirement retain the bounded tombstone through study end. The [entry guard](../scripts/rh_maker_late_flow_guard.py) already treats a qualifying delayed old-source print, including one tagged with a reconnect generation, as an execution-unknown old episode rather than a new quote fill. Passive exit has its own retired-ask check.

The implementation must process timers chronologically: activation due, inherited rest/hold/fallback requests, then cancellation due, with deadlines recomputed after every transition. Include hedge/exit/passive-buy timeouts and cancel late-flow reconciliation boundaries. Parent `>` timeouts need the first representable overdue instant (`timeout_ns + 1`); `>=` transitions use the exact deadline. Calling inherited tick on these ordered boundaries preserves capital/exposure/funding accounting over elapsed pieces. Reject backward receipts before mutation and make repeated ticks idempotent.

A cancellation can be generated *inside* inherited `tick` or an event callback. A post-`super().tick` pass alone is insufficient: [MakerBranch.tick](../scripts/rh_maker_engine.py:462) can request a historical rest cancel, then hit its confirmation timeout at lines 464–466 before returning. Override both `_request_cancel` and `_request_passive_cancel` to call the parent, preserve request metadata, and immediately settle a cancel already due at the active timer boundary. Otherwise schedule its future boundary. Apply existing transitions before inherited tick and newly generated ones afterwards.

A due time strictly before the next receipt still needs coverage checks over the intervening interval. The existing engines detect explicit invalidations and stale incoming source clocks but do not generally label every quiet interval a feed gap. Therefore the future clock branch needs an explicit freshness-expiry boundary while a quote/cancel reconciliation, inventory or intent is open. If a required venue's last valid source/receipt crosses the frozen age limit before a replacement book is exposed, mark `assumed_ack_coverage_gap_open_obligation`. A fresh incoming book cannot repair that already uncovered interval. This can still censor the scenario. Freshness does not prove that the public trade feed contains every execution; completeness remains a stated prerequisite, not an observed guarantee.

Later-received books whose **source is at/before the assumed ACK** and at/after the chosen anchor source can revise its premise, including equal source timestamps that do not establish ordering within the source clock's resolution. Predeclare unknown if relevant same-price depth or post-only state differs, rather than backdating a new queue after attribution. This is a restrictive consistency rule for the declared carry-forward assumption, not proof of actual queue priority. Source-after-due depth differences are normal; they cannot identify cancellations ahead versus behind.

Keep bounded exact duplicate-ID evidence across live and retired episodes. The frozen [entry tombstone](../scripts/rh_maker_late_flow_guard.py:20) has no seen-ID set, so an already processed print repeated after retirement can otherwise manufacture a late-flow unknown. The new wrapper drops validated duplicates before queue/tombstone attribution and retains exact evidence through study end; reaching the predeclared ID cap yields unknown instead of silently evicting IDs. Missing/malformed IDs retain explicit validation outcomes. Advance timers even when a duplicate is discarded.

Native IDs are **assumed** unique and stable across transport generations, not guaranteed by this note. Source/direction/price/quantity form the economic signature; first generation is retained separately, and an identical reconnect retransmission is logged as a duplicate. New prints still face inherited generation/invalidation checks. Null, boolean, container, negative numeric or unbounded IDs are invalid. Conflicting content withdraws net for every affected finalized episode, including full orders without remainder tombstones; retain bounded ID-to-episode provenance and stable `(episode_number, role)` ACK evidence rather than Python object identities. Caps or missing-ID evidence must still flag relevant retired obligations before stopping.

Historical adjudication continues after an unrelated branch halt: validated late raw RH revisions and genuinely new old-source entry/exit prints can invalidate an earlier completed episode. They cannot create new fills or cash. The implementation preserves the original branch halt reason while marking each affected old episode execution unknown. Both this rule and equal-receipt retirement are necessary to prevent cohort scoring from retaining falsely known zeroes or profits.

This proposal deliberately does **not** add a “cancel stays live until first later public book” completed-P&L branch by changing `cancel_due_ns`. That would also alter rest, timeout, and tombstone semantics. Strict v1 already records the first-book confirmation path and censors a print in its unresolved interval. A fully simulated late-cancel scenario would need a separate `assumed_cancel_effective_ns` and a targeted trade gate override for both quote directions, plus all the same late-print guards. It should be a second reviewed change if the clock-ACK sensitivity is informative. The public book is never a private cancel ACK in either case.

## Economics, invariants, and expected diagnostic value

Retain inherited FIFO lot accounting, entry/HL hedge, passive RH exit/HL buy or taker fallback, first eligible-book depth, partial fills, every own-notional fee, reserve, capital, funding-unknown state, and unmatched-inventory handling. Anchor/depth failures, off-grid dust, insufficient minimum quantity, partial IOC, rescue retries and unflattened inventory retain their loss or unknown consequences; a post-only rejection cannot erase existing passive-exit inventory. Target calibration selects a quote and is not a realized fee or a substitute for actual exit economics. Preserve the parent's USDG/USDC comparison assumption. Each scenario is an independent counterfactual ledger; no branch P&Ls are summed. Report completed net only for flat, fully reconciled episodes, withdrawing prior episode net if late tombstone evidence makes execution unknown. Use the all-admission denominator and show unknown counts separately from known no-flow zeroes.

The maker increment is attributed on **public trade receipt**, and its hedge/contingent-buy delays start there under inherited economics. This declares an information-delay assumption in addition to private order ACK latency; a real private fill notification could arrive differently. Public historical flow is also assumed unaffected by our counterfactual order. Neither is established by the capture. Activation wins an exact source-time tie under the declared model; cancellation ties remain unknown.

Each audit, episode and summary row has the scenario ID, assumed execution label and effective delays; transition rows also distinguish assumed effective time, timer boundary and the **actual outer processing receipt**. A delayed callback must not relabel its synthetic due boundary as a receipt. Anchor hashes and source/receipt times remain separate. Match cohorts by common admission/decision ID and source exposure, not just asset/size/tier: scenario fills and strict halts cause later admissions to diverge. Report common-time and unmatched exposure separately.

Focused synthetic invariants before a future freeze:

1. An RH book received before due can anchor at due; a trade with source and receipt after due but before the next RH book is processed against its same-price queue. A pre-due source print arriving late cannot fill. A book received after due cannot become the anchor.
2. The same callback's new book is never used for the earlier timer decision. A stale/missing anchor, crossed post-only proxy, generation change, or late pre-due contradictory book is labelled explicitly. No negative queue or quantity above the original order/eligible print volume is possible.
3. A print received after cancel due but sourced before it remains an old-quote obligation. A source-after-due print is excluded only in the declared clock-cancel scenario; equal-time ties and late retired-quote flow stay unknown. Test with no current quote and with a newer quote already open.
4. An entry partial and a passive-exit partial produce exactly the inherited hedge, cancel, fallback, fee, reserve, capital, and funding behavior. The class does not turn shallow IOC or open inventory into a completed zero. No book depth is shared twice across same-event intents.
5. On a fixture where due-time books agree with the pre-due anchors and there is no intervening flow, revision or coverage gap, clock-ACK and strict economic ledgers should agree. Receipt exactly at due alone is insufficient if that new book's queue differs, because the clock rule uses preceding information. Differences from the stopped pilot are reported as an **attrition decomposition**: strict activation-window censor, strict cancel-window censor, separate retirement correction, scenario known no-flow, scenario attributed partial/full flow, and remaining true feed/obligation unknowns.
6. Test a long receipt interval that creates a cancel inside inherited tick, repeated idempotent ticks, the exact callback that retires a quote, equal-receipt tombstones, reconnect generation, duplicate old prints, and raw versus accounting-depleted anchors. Verify the protocol's 18 source hashes unchanged.

### Concrete future implementation scope

The ACK subclass builds on the separately labelled retirement correction. Its nine method hooks are `__init__` (scenario/raw-anchor/ID evidence), `tick` (ordered boundaries and coverage), `_request_cancel`, `_request_passive_cancel` (internal due settlement), `process` (validation/duplicate ordering and anchor invalidation), `_on_book` (raw snapshot/revision check), `_close_episode_if_flat` (episode assumptions), `_log` (scenario label on every audit event), and `summary`. Helpers provide activation, cancellation, historical adjudication and next boundary selection for both directions. `_on_trade`, `_apply_fill`, `_fill_hedge`, `_fill_exit`, `_fill_passive_buy`, target selection and FIFO mechanics remain delegated. Focused synthetic fixtures cover the causal rules, partial loss exits, funding, rejection, caps, full-order conflicting IDs, and prior-halt evidence. No new capture or archive replay is launched by the module or this document.

The likely gain is longer measurable Standard exposure and fewer *simulator-created* due-to-next-book halts. It is not evidence that Standard would have filled or profited: the as-of-due queue and deterministic ACK are explicit, unverifiable execution assumptions. If completed outcomes change sign only under this scenario, the correct conclusion is sensitivity to unobserved execution timing, not a discovered arbitrage.
