# Cross-venue maker quoting and contingent hedging: research design

**Reviewed 2026-09-29 UTC.** This is a research design for Hyperliquid (HL), Lighter Core, and the Robinhood Chain Lighter domain (RH Lighter). It proposes no order placement or change to the existing paper monitor. The prior [maker feasibility review](maker-hedge-feasibility.md) and [exploratory replay](maker-roundtrip-exploratory.md) already establish that a public trade through a quote is only a *possible-flow signal*, not our fill. In the latter replay, the two entry-positive, complete conditional paths lost 7.97 and 9.39 bp after modeled fees. Those paths neither validate nor rule out the different policy below.

## What the literature supports

| Primary research | Result relevant here | Application and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Guilbaud and Pham, *Optimal High Frequency Trading with limit and market orders*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.5040), 2011 | Jointly controls passive quotes, market orders, and inventory; calibrates spread transitions and limit-order execution intensities, and tests execution priority. | The decision is whether to quote, hold inventory, or pay to hedge. Its fill intensities must be measured on these venues; its simulations are not our expected return. |
| [Huang, Lehalle, and Rosenbaum, *The queue-reactive model*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.0563), 2013/2014 | Uses event-level book updates and state-dependent order-flow intensities in a price-time queue. | Estimate *conditional* queue depletion and fill hazards by price, spread, imbalance, time of day, and volatility. A 30-second REST book cannot calibrate this model. |
| [Lalor and Swishchuk, *Market Simulation under Adverse Selection*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12721), 2024, revised 2026 | Tests a market-making simulator against futures data and finds that fill probabilities and price moves after adverse fills materially alter results; independent price and order simulation can inflate performance. | Couple fill scenarios to the subsequent hedge book and markout; do not multiply an unconditional spread by an independent fill probability. Futures evidence is a methodological warning, not measured HL/Lighter parameters. |
| [Barzykin et al., *Optimal Execution with Passive Market Impact*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28323), July 2026 preprint | Models the trade-off among quote distance, fill probability, price response to order flow, and non-execution; reports calibration evidence from NASDAQ equities and FX. | Compare quote depths and refresh rules only after estimating venue-specific arrival and fill behavior. This recent preprint is supporting research, not a validated crypto-perp calibration or performance guarantee. |
| [Hautsch, Scheuch, and Voigt, *Building Trust Takes Time: Limits to Arbitrage for Blockchain-Based Assets*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.00595), 2018, revised 2023 | Empirical Bitcoin order-book and transfer data link cross-exchange spreads to settlement delay and cross-exchange capital movement. | Pre-fund each leg and price transfer/collateral frictions. This is an analogy about cross-venue settlement risk; it does not measure today's HL–RH transfer times. |

An operational baseline is [Hummingbot's documented cross-exchange market-making strategy](https://hummingbot.org/strategies/v1-strategies/cross-exchange-market-making/) (page dated September 22, 2026): it derives maker quotes from the taker venue's executable price plus a required profit, cancels when that prospective profit or balance disappears, and hedges *actual* maker fills even if the later hedge is unfavorable. Its documented V1 connector support is spot CLOB/AMM; using the rule for perps would require separate margin, basis, funding, and liquidation controls. The existing fixed-best-maker, forced-five-second-exit probe is narrower than this adaptive quote-and-hedge policy.

## A testable policy, not an arbitrage certificate

At each synchronized observation, choose an economically equivalent **base quantity** `q`, an exchange-valid maker price and lot, and a hedge venue with enough *size-walked* opposing depth. For a maker **buy** with subsequent taker **sell**, the opening cash-flow difference is

`G_buy(q) = q × [VWAP_hedge_bid(q, fill time + hedge delay) − maker_bid]`.

For a maker **sell** with subsequent taker **buy**, it is

`G_sell(q) = q × [maker_ask − VWAP_hedge_ask(q, fill time + hedge delay)]`.

The prospective quote can only use information known before posting. For a **new RH long / HL short perp pair**, the completed-price condition is more precise than an opening spread:

`RH maker bid ≤ E[HL sell VWAP at fill+delay + RH exit bid − HL exit ask | maker fill, state, holding rule] − (all expected costs + risk charge + target profit)/q`.

This follows from opening with a RH buy and HL sell, then eventually selling RH and buying HL. For a **new RH short / HL long pair**, the signs reverse: `RH maker ask ≥ E[HL buy VWAP at fill+delay − HL exit bid + RH exit ask | maker fill, state, holding rule] + (all expected costs + risk charge + target profit)/q`. The expectations are trained only on past data and must include a predeclared flattening rule, basis distribution, delay, funding, margin, conversion, and four fee-bearing legs. Conditioning on the maker fill matters because informed flow can worsen both the immediate hedge and later basis. Setting the exit-basis term to zero is a strong, explicit assumption, not a property of a hedged perp pair. Current hedge depth is only an input; it is not the future execution or exit price. This adapts the [Hummingbot spot strategy](https://hummingbot.org/strategies/v1-strategies/cross-exchange-market-making/) operational rule to a perp liability; the spot rule alone does not establish completed perp economics. Reprice or cancel if hedgeable quantity, quote freshness, or conditional edge disappears. A fill can arrive while cancellation is pending, so the hedge policy must still handle it. For an actual partial fill, hedge only that increment and separately cancel or track the remaining maker quantity. Do not skip a loss-making hedge after a fill merely to keep an opening-spread statistic positive.

The **completed** strategy ledger must add the eventual exit of both positions, four actual fee-bearing notionals, funding, borrow or transfer costs, conversion, and residual inventory mark-to-market. A simple objective is

`max_quote,route E[completed P&L] − λ Var(completed P&L) − η E[|unhedged inventory| × exposure time]`,

subject to available margin, position limits, quote validity, and hedge capacity. In a stylized one-quote version, `E[completed P&L]` depends on `Pr(fill)` *and* `E[P&L | fill]`; these are correlated because aggressive flow that fills the maker may move the hedge book against us. No-fill carries capital and opportunity costs but yields no realized spread. Quote distance, cancellation threshold, and hedge urgency are controls to calibrate, not constants to optimize on the same sample used to score them.

Track a **state vector**, not just net delta: signed RH position `I_RH`, signed HL position `I_HL`, open-entry lots and prices, unrealized cross-venue basis, accrued funding, margin headroom at each venue, and residual unhedged delta `Δ = I_RH + I_HL` for equivalent base perps. An RH long and equal HL short have `Δ = 0` but still have **two open positions and a basis liability**; repeated matched pairs must count toward gross-position, margin, funding, and basis-risk limits. Use both gross-inventory and delta bands: skew quotes to reduce existing positions, shrink or cancel a side near a limit, and choose a faster hedge when `|Δ|`, volatility, or hedge-book depletion rises. A quote that **opens** a new pair uses the expected-exit condition above. A quote that **reduces** an existing lot is marked against that lot's actual remaining RH/HL entry and liability, using a predeclared FIFO convention, rather than credited as a fresh opening spread. A route score for each permissible hedge venue `j` should use the *future execution distribution*, not the lowest current top quote: `E[side-adjusted VWAP_j(q, t+δ_j) + fees_j + conversion_j + inventory-risk_j]`. An emergency hedge can sacrifice quoted margin to cap unhedged risk. If a hedge is rejected or partial, keep the residual in the ledger and apply a predeclared stop/flatten rule; do not count it as a completed pair.

### Venue-specific constraints

- [HL's book matches by price-time priority](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hypercore/order-book), while [ALO](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/order-types) is post-only and IOC can partially fill then cancel. A price touch or a level-size decline does not reveal an unplaced order's queue position. HL's [perp fee table](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/fees) lists native tier-0 maker **1.5 bp**, taker **4.5 bp** as of review; HIP-3 growth, deployer share, aligned collateral, account volume, and conditional maker rebates can alter particular routes. Do not assume a rebate without the account's effective schedule.
- [Lighter Core's account table](https://apidocs.lighter.xyz/docs/account-types) lists Standard **0/0 maker/taker** with **300 ms taker** and **300 ms cancel/modify** processing, versus base Premium **0.4/2.8 bp** with **140 ms taker** and lower maker/cancel latency. The [archived official RH-domain account table](../data/raw/comparators/rh_lighter/20260929T040726Z/sources/account-types.md) lists Standard **0/0**, **300 ms taker**, **200 ms cancel/modify**, versus base Premium **1.2/3.5 bp**, **200 ms taker**. These published processing figures exclude client/network delay and do not establish fill speed. Lower fee and lower latency are different account choices; recompute the route with the *actual* tier.
- On **RH Standard**, both maker and taker explicit fees are zero. Posting passively there seeks a better **execution price than crossing RH's spread**, not an RH fee saving. A completed RH-maker-in/RH-out plus HL-taker-in/HL-out path still has four fills: two native tier-0 HL taker fills alone cost roughly **9 bp of matched notional** if their notionals are similar, before reserve and slippage. An HIP-3 route with a *verified* 0.9 bp taker rate would instead cost roughly **1.8 bp** across its two HL taker fills; check each asset's deployer/growth/collateral settings rather than applying that figure to every market. Fee and latency tiers alter which quotes are placed, canceled, and filled. They cannot be evaluated solely by subtracting a different fee from an identical assumed fill set.
- [Robinhood's domain documentation](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain/lighter-domains/) says RH Lighter has its own sequencer, execution, blockspace, and liquidity, and documents **USDG** deposits. It is a separate hedge route from Lighter Core. For direct crypto perps, first establish identical base/contract payout and collateral conversion; USDC and USDG must not be silently treated as the same executable dollar. For RH stock tokens versus HL equity perps, token/share multiplier, issuer claims, trading windows, funding, and delivery mismatch make delta hedging an exposure management technique rather than a same-asset lock.
- A prefunded **spot/spot** maker fill changes token and cash inventory; a **perp/perp** hedge creates two margined positions with basis, funding, liquidation, and eventual unwind risk. A token/spot versus perp pair combines both. The strategy ledger must model the appropriate cash flow and exit for each route.

## First public-data tests and identification limit

**Priority: RH-domain maker → HL taker.** Robinhood Chain is new enough that sparse flow and a wider displayed spread are plausible; neither implies a profitable fill after the hedge. Following the user's size clarification, predeclare **$100, $250, $500, and $1,000** maker order budgets, with **$1,000 primary**. Sizes below $100 need supporting evidence before inclusion. Enforce each market's minimum order value, tick, and lot. Small quotes can improve hedge depth and possible-full-flow frequency, while the same queue ahead and fixed conversion/operating costs can overwhelm them. Report each size separately, including no-valid-order and no-flow cases. Price each RH maker quote from the HL *size-walked* hedge bid/ask and the **conditional full-exit-basis** allowance above; do not simply join RH's current best bid/ask. The reverse HL-maker → RH-hedge direction can be a separately frozen comparator. This RH-domain **perp CLOB** study is distinct from canonical Robinhood Chain Stock Token swaps on Uniswap or another AMM; the latter has token multiplier, gas, and spot/perp inventory economics and needs its own route specification.

1. **Freeze the universe and controls**: RH Lighter→HL **BTC and ETH** direct crypto perps as controls, and **NVDA and XAG** equity/metal perps as separate RWA cohorts *only after* matching their contract payout, size unit, trading availability, and collateral. RWA order flow, fees, oracle sessions, and exit basis can differ sharply from crypto; do not pool their outcomes or silently substitute a Stock Token for a perp. Add HL↔Core as an external comparator. Freeze all four sizes, maker quote rule, tick/lot handling, 0.5/1/3-second hypothetical arrival delays, hedge-delay scenarios, quote refresh/cancel thresholds, stale-feed limits, and all fees before a later observation window. Freeze a **ten-second exit-request deadline as the primary research policy**, with delayed actual executions and unresolved inventory retained, matching the current short-horizon question; longer 30/60-second horizons are separate sensitivities. Use chronological train/holdout blocks and report every considered quote, including no-flow and failed-hedge cases. Fee/latency scenarios must distinguish RH Standard (0/0 bp, 300 ms taker, 200 ms cancel), Plus (0.5/0.5 bp, 300 ms taker, 200 ms maker/cancel), and base Premium (1.2/3.5 bp, 200 ms taker, 0 ms maker/cancel) using the [archived September 26 official account table](../data/raw/comparators/rh_lighter/20260929T040726Z/sources/account-types.md). Published zero-millisecond *processing* is not zero client/consensus latency. For an RH-maker → HL-hedge route, the RH taker latency is relevant to a possible *RH-side emergency unwind*, while the primary hedge delay must be measured for HL order submission; cancellation latency is relevant to late RH fills.
2. **Record event time and queue evidence** using public BBO/L2 and trade streams, receipt timestamps, source timestamps, and Lighter sequence continuity. At a hypothetical arrival, place all displayed same-price size ahead of the proposed quote. Only opposite-aggressor trades at/through that price may advance a conservative queue calculation. Report touch, possible partial-flow, and possible full-flow separately. A trade-through remains counterfactual: adding our order could change flow, and hidden order/cancel/priority state is not reconstructed. The [existing feasibility review](maker-hedge-feasibility.md) identifies the specific venue feeds and gaps.
3. **Measure coupled adverse selection**: for each possible-flow event, set the earliest hedge decision to **local receipt of the flow confirmation plus** a modeled decision, network, and processing delay. An event's earlier exchange trade timestamp cannot trigger an action before its message is received. Use the first hedge-side book whose **local receipt and calibrated source time are both at or after that due time**, and whose source time is after the triggering trade; walk the exact partial quantity. If clocks cannot be aligned well enough for that dual check, classify the case as unknown rather than accepting an apparently fresh hedge quote. Report fill-flow-conditioned maker-to-hedge price move, 0.5/1/3/10-second hedge-side markouts, missing depth, stale books, and inventory exposure. Compare with all eligible quote times to show selection; do not call a hypothetical markout realized P&L.
4. **Compare frozen route policies** on the holdout: executable-hedge-derived adaptive quote/cancel versus fixed-best quote; immediate hedge request and a ten-second exit-request deadline as the primary research close, retaining delayed or unresolved actual executions. Evaluate a predeclared inventory-band alternative that may recycle exposure through later opposite-side fills at **30 and 60 seconds only as separate sensitivities**. Include basis/funding, mark-to-market, tail drawdown, and the independently frozen final flattening rule at either horizon. This is a **new holding policy**, never a reinterpretation of the existing ten-second paper monitor or an automatic recommendation to extend holding. Include processing-delay and spread-widening stress. Report full-flow *signal* rates and conditional hedge/exit paths, not a fill rate or investable return.

Public data can reject obviously uneconomic quotes and show how adverse-flow scenarios behave. It cannot identify `Pr(our order fills at its actual acknowledged queue place)`, true cancel/replace race, actual taker matching/rejection/slippage, or account-specific fees and margin. Those require authenticated order acknowledgements with exchange timestamps and IDs, private partial-fill/cancel events, measured client-to-exchange latency, fee and balance records, and complete funding/position ledgers. A future *separately authorized* small live calibration would be needed to estimate actual fill probability. No such calibration is part of this design.

## Atomic route boundary

[Uniswap v2's original whitepaper](https://docs.uniswap.org/whitepaper.pdf) describes a flash swap repaid within one **atomic transaction**; [CoW's settlement contract](https://github.com/cowprotocol/contracts/blob/main/src/contracts/GPv2Settlement.sol) likewise groups on-chain trades/interactions in a settlement call that reverts if one trade fails. A same-chain AMM or solver route can therefore sometimes enforce an all-or-nothing outcome across compatible pools or contracts. That property is limited to actions under one settlement environment and compatible assets; it does not make a future passive CLOB fill and subsequent hedge atomic. HL, Lighter Core, and RH Lighter have distinct matching/settlement domains; RH's [domain specification](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain/lighter-domains/) explicitly separates its sequencer and liquidity from Core, while USDG conversion or cross-chain transfer is an additional path. Posting on one book and hedging later on another is therefore **sequential inventory-taking**, with fill, price, delay, collateral, and unwind risk. A visually positive cross-venue spread cannot be described as risk-free atomic arbitrage.
