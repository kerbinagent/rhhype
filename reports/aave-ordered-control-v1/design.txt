Control: omit the one transaction preceding the fixed first liquidation

The original ordered replay is complete and independently audited. It
reproduced both real receipts and exposed two native transfers. The prefix
emits an AnswerUpdated-shaped event. The present question is whether the
same original liquidation, with original block context, also succeeds without
the prefix. This control was selected after inspecting that positive replay;
it is not an untouched prospective strategy sample or an economic backtest.

Copy the exact retained eth_simulateV1 params from the successful positive
run. Remove only calls[0]; the original calls[1] remains unchanged. Preserve
the parent block number and all block overrides, original sender, destination,
calldata, value, gas limit, fees, nonce, access list and chain ID. Validation
and traceTransfers remain true. No state overrides, funding changes, signatures
or transaction submission. Do not optimize fees or introduce another call.

Fixed maximum five RPCs against https://ethereum-rpc.publicnode.com:
1 eth_chainId, must be1.
2 Parent header by retained number, must match retained hash/state/context.
3 Exactly one eth_simulateV1, original liquidation without its prefix.
4 Parent header recheck.
5 Target header and original two-transaction prefix recheck.
No alternate block, target, provider, retry, transport fallback or rerun.

The observed control outcomes are distinct:
- status0 with no logs: reverted_without_prefix; retain returnData and strictly
  decode a canonical Error(string) if present. Gas is simulated and not spent.
- status1, original gas and regular logs: same_receipt_reproduced_without_prefix.
- status1 with other gas/logs: succeeded_with_different_effects.
Transport/RPC/schema/context/cap failures are unavailable and never counted
as an observed EVM revert. Both success and revert still require canonical
header rechecks. One-call result must preserve the target block context.
Successful calls require a logs array; failed-call API responses may omit it.
Failed calls require a nonempty structured error; missing error is unavailable.
Any present synthetic native events are strictly decoded before comparison.

A revert with the prefix absent, versus matched success with it present,
would show dependence on that prefix under the provider's historical model.
It does not alone identify which internal prefix state change is responsible,
independently prove the oracle's identity/wiring, or establish our ability to
capture a liquidation. There is no economic accounting in this control.
