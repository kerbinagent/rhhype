SINGLE-VENUE SHORT-HORIZON RESEARCH — FIRST CYCLE
Completed 2026-10-01, approximately 20:00 UTC. Public-data paper research only.

Question
Can a price difference identify a profitable short trade on just one venue?
More specifically, can an unusually large trade temporarily dislocate a thin
venue while a reference venue stays stable?

Research
Cross-venue price discovery and temporary order-flow impact are documented
mechanisms. They do not establish that our venues, fees, data delays or chosen
signals offer a profitable trade. Large orders can also carry information, and
liquidity can recover without the traded price returning to its old level.
Sources and applicability limits are in reports/single-venue-research-sources.json.

Experiment
Three fixed rules: gap fade, leader follow, and local liquidity-shock fade.
Each rule/venue pair has its own independent paper ledger, one perpetual
position at a time, at most $100 actual entry notional, $600 starting collateral,
400 ms minimum order delay, native quantity/price rules and minimum order sizes.
The quantity calculation leaves 1% room below the notional limit. Each holding
period targets ten seconds from entry; subsequent book arrival and exit delay
extend actual holding time. Entry IOC limit: 1 bp; exit IOC limit: 10 bp.
No stop-loss guarantee. Partial and unresolved executions remain explicit.
Published Standard zero fees are checked against archived market metadata.
RH latency is an archived model assumption, not a fresh verification.
Capital uses 5% annualized held notional; a separate 5 bp allowance stresses costs.
Ledger cash stays in its own collateral. Cross-venue price comparison assumes
USDG/USDC parity. The independent ledgers must not be added as one portfolio.

The shock rule requires a completed one-second burst at least five times its
past median nonempty-second notional, strongly one-sided flow, a substantial
fraction of nearby depth, a local price move, stable reference, an aligned
relative-price deviation and enough room after estimated round-trip costs.
The thresholds are uncalibrated research hypotheses, not measured probabilities.

Historical exploration: three preselected ten-minute LIT captures
1. One Core short gained $0.068524 after fees/capital, or $0.019042 after stress.
   Gap and leader rules selected the SAME event; it is one unique success.
2. Gap-fade trades lost $0.121586 on Core and $0.027920 on RH. Another entry
   did not fill. The leader rule made no entry.
3. Four entry attempts produced no fills. No completed trade.
All three independent raw-event audits passed. The shock rule traded none.
Across the old captures, 47 unusually large flow bursts were observed. Of these,
46 failed the trading cost hurdle; rejection categories overlap. The old
captures have ordinary trade data for only one venue apiece, although both books
are present. Their lack of other-venue trades is missing coverage, not inactivity.

Fresh prospective sample
Protocol frozen at f0b90a0 before capture. Capture ran 19:46:50.507215 through
19:56:51.262773 UTC (600.756 seconds), both trade feeds and books, no recorded
errors, 15,087 wire records and 1,830,908 compressed raw bytes.
Core gap fade: two trades, both losses; total cash after fees/capital -$0.040428,
or -$0.139406 after the separate stress allowance. Both positions finished flat.
All five other rule/venue ledgers had zero entries. No execution unknowns.
Independent audit reproduced two signals, four orders, four depth executions
and both closed cash ledgers. None of these are authenticated private fills.

Fresh shock diagnostics
There were 52 large Core flow bins and three large RH bins. None passed the
complete shock rule. On Core, 47/52 failed the depth-fraction condition and
43/52 lacked the required local move; every burst failed the cost hurdle.
Of 49 Core bursts with a matched ten-second future observation, fading the
trade direction had a mean midpoint return of -0.233 bp, before any costs;
21 were positive. Three RH observations averaged +2.295 bp, far too few for a
conclusion. Missing targets are retained. Events overlap and are dependent.
These are price diagnostics, not executed cash returns or evidence of causality.

Interpretation and next research
A large trade relative to recent activity is often small relative to available
liquidity. LIT alone has not established the proposed thin-book mechanism.
The next comparison will include other assets and retain LIT as a control.
The existing signal and execution rules should remain fixed for that comparison;
asset selection must not be based on whichever historical trade made money.
Further work can examine faster burst detection and an additional independent
reference venue. The current one-second detector may miss faster reversals.
No repeatably profitable one-venue strategy is established by this first cycle.

Evidence preservation
The original historical replay hit its trace-size cap before any completed
trade and remains preserved. Supplement 512a20c stores hashes of full raw books
instead of duplicate depth in each trace. Full books remain in original captures
and the audit reconstructs them. Synthetic tests verify identical economics and
reject changed depth or corrupted fill values. Trading rules were unchanged.
Large-flow diagnostic 5c2532a was specified after initial historical exploration,
before the prospective endpoint and before reading its economic outcomes.
